# Clay Tone of Voice Guide

*Built 2026-07-21 from a 12-page scrape of clay.com (homepage, /claygent, /waterfall-enrichment, /pricing, /careers, 2 customer stories, 3 blog posts, changelog, docs), Clay's own "Behind the scenes of Clay's new homepage" post, and their published job descriptions. Every quoted example is verbatim from the live site. Structure follows the best public voice guides (Mailchimp, GitLab, Atlassian, Monzo, Polaris).*

**Status key:** everything quoted is CONFIRMED (scraped). Interpretations are labeled where they're judgment calls.

---

## 1. Why this guide exists

So anyone writing "as Clay" (landing pages, outreach, demo collateral) sounds like the same company the prospect just saw on clay.com. It gives decision rules, so when two phrasings both seem fine, the guide picks the winner.

**One-line voice summary:** Clay writes like a confident engineer talking to another builder: imperative, contraction-heavy, second person, comfortable with fragments. Named customers with hyper-specific numbers carry all the hype. The villain is always manual work and single-source data; Clay never names a competitor.

---

## 2. The voice: three traits

Clay's own words for their target voice (from their Founding Content Designer and Brand Designer job posts): *"trustworthy yet creative, powerful yet human"*; *"distinctly human, confident, intentionally creative and ever curious, while maintaining clarity, usability, and conversion"*; *"the brand is felt, not explained."*

Distilled into three working traits, each with its failure mode:

| Trait | We are… | …but never | In practice |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Builder-confident** | Direct, imperative, technically fluent. We assume the reader wants to build something. | Arrogant, hypey, salesy | Verb-first headlines ("Build systems to grow revenue"). Engineering vocabulary: infrastructure, orchestrate, deploy, systems, plays. Never "revolutionary," "cutting-edge," or "best-in-class." |
| **Specific to the point of absurdity** | Numbers, names, mechanisms. Proof is a named customer, a metric, and *how they did it*. | Vague, rounded-off, unattributed | "Mistral AI cut the time to map their TAM and score accounts from 2 months to 10 days." The mechanism clause is never omitted. If we don't have the number, we cut the claim rather than fake one. |
| **Playful, quietly** | Warm, human, a wink where earned. The play lives mostly in the *visuals* (clay machines, ball pits); the copy stays composed. | Jokey, emoji-laden, cute for its own sake | A rhetorical-question headline is as loose as product copy gets: "Why use one contact database when you can use them all?" Sign-off flourish: "Inspired by our customers. Built with love." No exclamation marks in marketing headlines, ever. |

## 3. Voice vs. tone: the dial per surface

Voice is constant; tone varies with the reader's state of mind (Mailchimp's rule). Clay demonstrably does this. Confirmed register shifts across their own site:

| Surface | Tone dial | Evidence from clay.com |
|---|---|---|
| **Homepage / product pages** | Tightest, most confident. Imperative fragments, zero emoji. | "Mine the web for custom data points to research and qualify accounts. Constantly monitor accounts for reasons to engage." |
| **Pricing** | Patient, transparent, almost support voice. First-person "we," disarming honesty. | "Clay charges customers exactly for the tokens consumed with no markup." |
| **Case studies** | Journalistic. Opens on a pull-quote, bolded result sentences, the customer's own words carry the hype. | "How Merge books 15% more enterprise meetings by researching 1,500 key accounts weekly with Clay" |
| **Blog (founder/practitioner)** | Candid, "we," analogies, admits past mistakes. | "Before automation, Clay's paid strategy had three issues most B2B teams will recognize." |
| **Blog (SEO/tactical)** | Loosest. Emoji allowed (only here), listicles, tables. | "handle the heavy lifting. 🦾" |
| **Careers / community** | Warmest and most human. | "Any of these catch your eye?" / "We can't wait to jam with you" |
| **Docs / changelog** | Plain, friendly-utilitarian, benefit per bullet. | "Hello, how can we help?" |

**Where our pre-demo landing pages sit:** between homepage and pricing. Homepage's builder confidence and specificity, plus pricing's warmth and honesty, because this reader already said yes to a demo. Reassure and prime them; don't re-sell. No emoji. Direct address by first name is fine, since the page is literally addressed to one person.

## 4. Writing mechanics (the rules)

1. **Second person, always.** "You," "your reps," "your stack." First-person "we" for Clay when explaining or hosting ("We'll come with a Retool-shaped table already loaded").
2. **Contractions everywhere**, including headlines: "The data other providers can't find, but Claygent can."
3. **Verb-first imperatives.** Headlines and CTAs open on the verb: Build, Create, Launch, Orchestrate, Scale, Enrich, Try, Explore.
4. **Fragments are legal** in product copy when they're imperative and rhythmic (three in a row is the house pattern).
5. **8–16 words per marketing sentence.** Blog can run longer but stays conversational.
6. **Setup–payoff punctuation:** Clay uses colons and em-dashes for the reveal. "The result: acquire and retain customers with more precision and creativity than ever."
7. **No exclamation marks** in headlines or buttons. (They appear only in careers and blog body copy.)
8. **Bold the proof inside prose:** "**Anthropic** 3x'd their enrichment rate…" Bold numbers and names, never adjectives.
9. **Present tense, active voice.** The old way gets past tense; Clay gets present.

## 5. Vocabulary

**Words Clay leans on (use these):**
- *Build / builders / building*: the verb the whole brand hangs on
- *Systems* ("Build systems to grow revenue," "GTM systems that compound")
- *Plays* ("Launch new plays as fast as you have ideas")
- *GTM* as an adjective: GTM plays, GTM stack, GTM engineering, GTM data layer
- *Infrastructure* and *development environment* (the IDE analogy: "GTM teams need their own place to build AND run growth systems")
- *Orchestrate / orchestration*, *workflows*, *signals*, *waterfall*, *enrichment*, *coverage*, *at scale*
- *Reps* (never "salespeople"), *GTM engineers*, *GTM teams*, *RevOps*, *ops*
- Internal-flavor terms, used sparingly outward: *Claymates* (employees), *the Clay crew*, *Claygent*

**Words Clay avoids (banned):**
- "Revolutionary," "cutting-edge," "game-changing," "best-in-class," "synergy," "solutions"
- "Leverage" as a verb (rare on their site; prefer "use")
- "Leads database" and "sales intelligence platform." Clay always frames itself as infrastructure, an environment, a system.
- Naming competitors in copy. The enemy is **the old way**: single providers, silos, stale CSVs, manual research. ("Stop relying on one tool to find emails and phone numbers.")

## 6. Proof: the Clay formula

Clay doesn't do generic social proof. The house pattern, everywhere on the homepage:

> **[Bold company]** + past-tense multiplier verb + specific metric + mechanism clause.

- "**Intercom** grew outbound pipeline 140% by continuously researching target accounts for headcount change, technographics, and web intent."
- "**Canva** saved 4 hrs/rep/week…"
- "**Vanta** cut post-call follow-up time from 3 days to <1…"

Rules: the mechanism clause ("by …ing") is never omitted; quotes carry name, title, and logo; scale claims stay current. As of July 2026: **"500,000+ leading GTM teams," "$5B valuation," "200+ data providers," "140M monthly Claygent runs."** (The older 5,000-customers / $3.1B / 150-providers numbers in `clay_value_prop.txt` are stale. Don't use them.)

Honesty is part of the voice. Clay admits limits in its own copy ("While AI tools aren't foolproof…"). If a claim can't be attributed, soften it or cut it.

## 7. Headline and CTA formulas

**Headlines (all patterns live on clay.com):**
1. Verb-first imperative + outcome: "Build systems to grow revenue"
2. Persona-as-subject: "GTM engineers build on Clay"
3. Capability contrast with the old way: "The data other providers can't find, but Claygent can"
4. Rhetorical question (max one per page): "Why use one contact database when you can use them all?"
5. Case-study formula: "How [Company] [result + number] by [mechanism] with Clay"
6. Two-line headline with the second line in the accent color (a visual formula, from the homepage feature cards)

**CTAs:**
- Primary: verb + the specific thing + "for free" where true. "Start free trial," "Try 20+ waterfalls for free," "Enrich your work emails for free." The CTA restates *the specific job of its section*, never a generic "Learn more."
- Secondary: "Explore [noun]". "Explore data marketplace," "Explore agents."
- Sentence case, no exclamation, arrow → allowed.
- For our demo-prep pages: "Confirm my demo time" fits the pattern (verb + specific thing). Good.

## 8. Do / don't rewrites (calibrated for the prospect landing pages)

| ❌ Don't | ✅ Do | Why |
|---|---|---|
| "Clay is a revolutionary AI-powered data platform." | "Clay waterfalls through 200+ data providers until the gaps fill in." | Mechanism over adjective. |
| "Join thousands of happy customers!" | "**Anthropic** 3x'd their enrichment rate with Clay's data marketplace." | Named proof with a number and a mechanism; no exclamation. |
| "Learn more" | "Explore agents" / "See what we'll build" | The CTA names the specific thing. |
| "We're excited to show you our industry-leading solution." | "15 minutes, no slides. We build in a live Clay table with Retool as the example." | Show, don't tell. Make a concrete promise. |
| "Retool's cutting-edge internal tooling is amazing and we love it." | Use Retool as the *working example* in the demo agenda instead of flattering it. | Personalization means relevance, not flattery. Never invent facts about the prospect. |
| "Say goodbye to ZoomInfo!" | "Stop relying on one tool to find emails and phone numbers." | The villain is the old way, never a named competitor. |

## 9. TL;DR (tape this above the keyboard)

- Write to a **builder**, in **second person**, **verb-first**, with **contractions**.
- Every claim gets a **number, a name, and a mechanism**, or it gets cut.
- The enemy is **manual work and single-source data**. Never name a competitor.
- **No exclamation marks. No emoji** (outside the SEO blog). No "revolutionary."
- Playfulness lives in the **visuals**; the copy stays composed, with at most one wink per page.
- Vocabulary: **build, systems, plays, GTM, orchestrate, reps, signals, waterfall.**
- Proof points (July 2026): 500,000+ GTM teams · $5B valuation · 200+ providers · 4.9★ G2.
- These landing pages = homepage confidence + pricing-page warmth. The reader already booked the demo. Prime, don't re-sell.
